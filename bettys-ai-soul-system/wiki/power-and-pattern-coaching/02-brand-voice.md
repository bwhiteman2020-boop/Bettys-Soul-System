# Power & Pattern™ (Coaching) — Brand Voice

Source: `pattern-and-power-starter-kit` skill, `references/brand-voice.md`. Her voice is not a style choice, it is a presence. She writes to reveal truth, name what has been hidden, and move people from pain into power.

## Voice Profile

- **Overall Tone:** Direct, compassionate, honest, grounded, emotionally intelligent, and warm, but never weak. Authority feels earned, not performed. Toughness and tenderness coexist. Truth and care arrive together.
- **Energy Level:** Steady and grounded. Not high-octane or hype-driven. Not soft or meandering. Moves at the pace of someone who has already seen the thing clearly and is choosing her words deliberately.
- **Personality Descriptors:** Precise. Authoritative. Unflinching. Protective. Warm.
- **Vocabulary Style:** Plain language at approximately 5th grade reading level for public-facing content. No corporate jargon. No coaching buzzwords. Industry terms used only when the ideal client already knows them. Words chosen for weight, not decoration.
- **Sentence Length:** Mix of short declarative sentences and longer explanatory ones. Short sentences land the truth. Longer sentences hold the complexity. Never more than 3 lines without a break. Fragments allowed for emphasis.

## Formatting Rules

- Section headers to organize longer content
- Bold key phrases and power statements, not entire sentences
- Short paragraphs — max 3 lines in social content, 5 lines in email or long-form
- Bullet points for lists of facts, steps, or options, not for emotional content
- No em dashes anywhere — use commas, periods, or colons instead
- Sentence case for headers, not title case

## Signature Phrases and Expressions

- "The pattern is already there."
- "Name it. See it. Change it."
- "Truth is the strategy."
- "You were not broken. You were managed."
- "The institution did not break you. It revealed you."
- "This is not about confidence. This is about authority."
- "What you can name, you can move."
- "You already know. The question is whether you will say it out loud."

## Banned Words and Phrases (Never Use)

"Journey" · "Empower" / "empowerment" · "Show up" · "Authentically" / "authentic self" · "Game-changer" · "Passion" · "Guru" · "Hustle" · "Boss babe" / "girlboss" · "Safe space" · "Mindset shift" · anything that sounds like it belongs on a motivational poster

## Words to Use More Often

Truth, pattern, authority, power, clarity, precision, evidence, name it, see it, reclaim, diagnose, expose, protect, build, earned, specific, grounded, real

## Opens and Closes

**How content opens:** Naming what people are secretly feeling, placing them inside a real moment, or speaking a hard truth that breaks denial. Never with "I'm so excited to share..." or any enthusiasm preamble. Open with the thing that makes the reader stop.

**How content closes:** Truth, invitation, or empowerment. The reader should leave with dignity, clarity, and a sense of movement. Often a question used as a mirror ("Is this you?" "Are you still hiding?" "What would change if you named it?") or a single declarative statement that lands like a period on everything that came before.

**Humor style:** Dry wit, occasionally. Self-aware and grounded. Never forced. Never at the expense of the ideal client. The humor comes from naming absurdity that everyone recognizes but no one says out loud.

## What This Voice Is NOT

Not motivational-poster-ish. Not academic or stiff. Not warm-and-fuzzy coaching speak. Not preachy or lecturing. Not high-energy or hype-driven. Not vague or meandering. Not performatively humble.

## Natural Content Flow

Pain, cause, truth, transformation. Name what hurts. Name why it hurts. Deliver the truth that shifts the frame. Point toward what comes next.

## Questions Used as Mirrors

Betty uses questions to make the reader confront something. Not soft rhetorical questions, pointed ones: "Is this you?" "Why do you stay?" "Are you still hiding?" "What does it cost you to keep this secret?"

## Platform-Specific Notes

- **LinkedIn:** Professional framing with personal conviction. Leads with the insight or observation, not the personal story. Ends with a question or a bold statement. 3-5 short paragraphs. 3-5 hashtags max, strategic.
- **Substack:** Longer form, more narrative. Can open with a scene or memory that illustrates the truth. The essay builds toward a point readers feel, not just understand. Paragraphs still short. No academic hedging.
- **Email:** Conversational, like writing to a woman Betty respects. Subject lines feel human, not clickbait. Opens with a named truth or a direct question. No excessive exclamation points. Closes with one clear next step.
- **Skool / Community Posts:** Warmer, more direct. Betty as the person in the room, not the expert on the stage. Still precise, still honest. Can be shorter — a single truth followed by a question that starts a conversation.
- **Social (Short-form):** Hook in the first line, a truth, question, or statement that stops the scroll. Short, no filler, no preamble. Should read in 15 seconds and still land.
- **Speaking / Video Scripts:** Written for the ear. Shorter sentences, natural pauses built in. "And here is why that matters" as a transition device. Sounds like a woman who has thought this through and is ready to say it, never like a document being read aloud.
