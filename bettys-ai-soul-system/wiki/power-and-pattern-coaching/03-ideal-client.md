# Power & Pattern™ (Coaching) — Ideal Client

Source: `pattern-and-power-starter-kit` skill, `references/ideal-client.md`. Always use "ideal client" — never "audience," "target audience," or "customer segment."

## Who She Is

A high-performing Black woman, typically 40 to 65, who has spent decades being the most competent person in rooms that refused to confirm it. She has built a track record that should speak for itself, but something keeps happening. She gets overlooked, overruled, undermined, or outright ignored. She has been told she is "too much," "too direct," or "intimidating." She knows something is wrong with that framing. She just cannot always name it clearly enough to fight back.

**What she calls herself:** A professional, a leader, an executive, a consultant, a business owner. Not "stuck" — "frustrated." She doesn't say she needs coaching, she says she needs someone who actually understands what she is dealing with.

**How Betty addresses her in content:** "You." Direct address. Sometimes "the women I work with." Never generic terms like "ladies" or "girlfriends." Treated as a professional, not a community member looking for belonging.

## Demographics

- Age range: 40-65
- Gender: Black women; some content resonates with women of color broadly
- Location: US-primary; some international (UK, Canada, Caribbean)
- Income: $75K-$250K+ individual income; often high earners relative to their environment
- Education: College-educated; many hold advanced degrees, certifications, or executive credentials
- Family situation: Varied — many are caregivers (children, aging parents); many are in relationships where their professional identity is not fully understood

## Psychographics

- Core values: Excellence, integrity, loyalty, impact, legacy
- Personality traits: High-achieving, analytical, perceptive, patient to a fault, excellent at reading rooms, prone to absorbing blame that does not belong to them
- Worldview: Believes in working hard and doing right, and is starting to understand the game was not designed for those rules to benefit her

## Pain

**#1 Pain Point:** She is performing at the highest level and being treated as if she is not. She has the receipts, the results, and the relationships, and somehow someone with less keeps getting more. She cannot fully explain why. That inability to name it is its own kind of torture.

**Secondary pain points:**
- Ideas dismissed in the room, then credited to someone else
- Constant code-switching, exhausting
- Told her directness is a problem when colleagues get celebrated for the same behavior
- Passed over, moved sideways, or managed out of spaces she built
- Starting to doubt her own perception of reality — terrifying

**How she describes her pain, in her words:**
- "I know what I'm seeing, but I can't prove it."
- "I'm always the one cleaning up everyone else's mess."
- "I've been here longer than all of them. I should be the one in that seat."
- "I'm tired of explaining myself to people who already made up their minds."
- "I don't even know why I'm still here."
- "I keep shrinking myself and it's still not enough."
- "I've done everything right. So why does this keep happening?"

## What She Has Already Tried (And Why It Didn't Work)

Executive coaching focused on "executive presence" (felt like being told to perform whiteness); leadership programs that didn't name race or gender as variables; HR complaints that went nowhere or made things worse; switching companies only to find the same pattern in a new building; reading every leadership book with diminishing returns; staying quiet, staying longer, working harder.

**Why it didn't work:** These treated the environment as neutral and the woman as the variable. They focused on adjusting her behavior inside a system designed to resist her. No one named the pattern or handed her the framework for what she was actually dealing with.

## What Success Looks Like to Her

Naming exactly what is happening to her without self-doubt. A strategy that works within reality, not inside a myth of meritocracy. Being recognized for what she actually produces. Building something of her own that cannot be taken or minimized. Feeling like the most powerful version of herself again. Not having to explain her excellence to anyone.

**Her biggest dream / desired identity:** To be undeniable. To be impossible to overlook, undervalue, or manage out. To be the authority in her space, in her own name, on her own terms.

## Where She Hangs Out Online

LinkedIn (professional updates, industry news, occasional venting in comments), Facebook groups (professional Black women's communities, alumni networks), Instagram (follows thought leaders), podcasts (Black professional women, leadership, business, personal development), Substack (increasingly, looking for long-form thinking that respects her intelligence).

## What Makes Her Laugh

Someone naming the specific absurdity of her situation out loud, the thing that's so true it almost hurts. Dry observations about institutional dysfunction, the specific comedy of being the most competent person in a chaotic organization, shared recognition of things that happen to Black professional women that everyone pretends don't happen. No softness wrapped around the humor needed — she needs someone else to finally say it.

## Objections Before Buying

- "I don't know if anyone can really understand my specific situation."
- "I've tried coaching before and it felt like I was being taught to manage myself better for them."
- "I'm not sure I have time to add one more thing."
- "What if naming this out loud makes it worse?"
- "I need to know this is worth the investment."

## What Makes Her Trust Betty

Betty has been inside the institution, not just consulting from the outside. She names exactly what the client has been experiencing without requiring her to explain it. She doesn't ask her to soften her edges or adjust for the room. She has a framework, a real one, for what is happening and what to do about it. She treats the client as the expert on her own experience and builds the strategy from there.
