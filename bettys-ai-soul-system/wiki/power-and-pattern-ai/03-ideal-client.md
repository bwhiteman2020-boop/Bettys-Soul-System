# Power and Pattern AI — Ideal Client Avatars

Source: `power-and-pattern-ai-profile` skill. Three avatars, all reachable through the same offer ladder (`04-programs-and-offers.md`).

## Avatar 1: The Overwhelmed HR Leader

- **Age:** 42-55, any gender. Bachelor's+ in HR/business/org development.
- **Values:** fairness, employee experience, professionalism, trust, efficiency, consistency, protecting the org.
- **Salary:** ~$100K-$200K+; org revenue $10M-several hundred million.
- **Industries:** mid-sized companies, nonprofits, healthcare, professional services, education, associations.
- **Tools:** Microsoft 365, Teams, SharePoint, Workday, BambooHR, ADP, UKG, Google Workspace, Slack, HRIS, LMS, ChatGPT/Copilot.
- **Pain:** repeated questions, scattered info (SharePoint/PDFs/email/drives/heads), process dependent on specific people, manual onboarding, hard-to-find policies, managers constantly asking HR, ungoverned AI experimentation, no clear AI value picture.
- **Beliefs to dispel:** needs huge tech budget first; processes "aren't ready" for AI; must know exactly what she wants before talking to a consultant; AI means replacing employees; Copilot/ChatGPT alone solves it; needs org-wide strategy before improving one workflow.
- **Gets info from:** SHRM, LinkedIn, HR conferences, peers, associations, HR tech vendors, Microsoft/tech partners, newsletters, consultants, webinars.
- **Alternative solvers:** large consulting firms, HR tech consultants, Microsoft partners, automation agencies, internal IT, AI consulting firms, software vendors, existing HRIS provider.
- **Best fit offers:** Operations Assessment (entry) → AI Agent & App Build (e.g., HR Knowledge Agent, Policy Assistant, Onboarding Agent, Manager Copilot).
- **Buys because:** (1) starts with her operations, not a predetermined tool; (2) can move from recommendation to actual build; (3) balances efficiency with governance, data, employee, and oversight concerns.

## Avatar 2: The Operations Leader Who Knows the Business Is Too Manual

- **Age:** 38-58, any gender. Background in business/operations/finance/tech/engineering/public admin.
- **Values:** efficiency, accountability, reliability, measurable results, ownership, continuous improvement.
- **Salary:** ~$120K-$250K+; company revenue $20M-$1B+.
- **Industries:** professional services, associations, nonprofits, healthcare admin, financial services, manufacturing, logistics, education, multi-location businesses.
- **Tools:** Microsoft 365, Google Workspace, Salesforce, HubSpot, Monday.com, Asana, Jira, SharePoint, Teams, Slack, ERPs, CRMs, Zapier, Power Automate.
- **Pain:** duplicate data entry across systems, heavy spreadsheet reliance, tribal knowledge, manual reporting, inconsistent department processes, staff constantly copying/summarizing/routing info, pressure for efficiency without headcount, unclear which AI use cases deserve investment.
- **Beliefs to dispel:** must automate everything; needs to replace the whole tech stack; IT should already know how; needs perfect data first; best start is buying AI licenses for everyone; only worth doing if it saves millions; AI strategy must be an enterprise-wide initiative.
- **Gets info from:** LinkedIn, industry associations, ops communities, HBR, McKinsey/Deloitte/Accenture/Gartner, Microsoft, tech vendors, conferences, peers, business podcasts, COO/ops newsletters.
- **Alternative solvers:** management consulting firms, digital transformation firms, automation consultants, internal IT, Microsoft/Salesforce partners, business process consultants, AI/low-code development agencies.
- **Best fit offers:** Operations Assessment → AI Agent & App Build → AI Operating System (full progression path).
- **Buys because:** (1) starts with "where is operational friction?" not "which AI product can we sell you?"; (2) identifies opportunities then builds custom solutions around actual workflows; (3) connects people, process, knowledge, and technology instead of treating AI as an isolated tool.

## Avatar 3: The Public-Sector Modernization Leader

- **Age:** 40-60, any gender. Background in public administration, criminal justice, business, IT, org leadership, or policy.
- **Values:** public service, integrity, accountability, security, transparency, responsible innovation, stewardship of taxpayer resources.
- **Salary:** ~$90K-$200K+ for senior roles; budgets range from millions to hundreds of millions.
- **Industries:** local/county/municipal government, public safety admin, law enforcement admin ops, emergency management, public utilities, education agencies.
- **Tools:** Microsoft 365, SharePoint, Teams, records/case management systems, learning platforms, internal databases, Power BI, legacy systems, specialized public-sector applications.
- **Pain:** staffing shortages, outdated processes, heavy documentation volume, info trapped in PDFs/drives/databases/retiring employees' heads, training demands, complex procurement, cybersecurity/sensitive-data concerns, pressure to modernize while keeping public trust, uncertainty about acceptable generative-AI use.
- **Beliefs to dispel:** "government can't use AI yet"; must wait until every policy question is resolved; AI is only for private companies; needs an enormous budget to start; old systems block all innovation; first initiative must be agency-wide; should ban AI entirely until fully understood.
- **Gets info from:** government professional associations, ICMA, public-sector tech conferences, Microsoft government resources, state/federal guidance, peer agencies, CIOs, legal counsel, cybersecurity teams, gov tech publications.
- **Alternative solvers:** large government contractors, public-sector tech consulting firms, Microsoft partners, internal IT, management consulting firms, systems integrators, specialized government software vendors.
- **Best fit offers:** Operations Assessment (customized for public sector) → AI Agent & App Build → AI Operating System. Potential solutions: policy assistants, SOP knowledge agents, training assistants, administrative workflow apps, internal knowledge systems, reporting support tools.
- **Buys because:** (1) can start with controlled operational/administrative use cases rather than high-risk decision automation; (2) builds in governance, data, human oversight, adoption, and workflow design alongside the tech; (3) moves assessment → prototype → implementation, letting the agency prove value incrementally.
